## 1. Business Model Canvas (BMC)

### 1.1 Core Essence

**Creator**: Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur
**Publication**: "Business Model Generation" (2010)

The Business Model Canvas is a strategic management and entrepreneurial tool that presents a one-page visual framework capturing the essential elements of a business plan. It allows you to describe, design, challenge, invent, and pivot your business model systematically.

**Core Philosophy**: Visual storytelling for business models, enabling rapid iteration and clear communication of value creation mechanisms.

### 1.2 The 9 Building Blocks

1. **Customer Segments** - Who are we creating value for?
2. **Value Propositions** - What value do we deliver to the customer?
3. **Channels** - How do we reach our customer segments?
4. **Customer Relationships** - What type of relationship does each customer segment expect?
5. **Revenue Streams** - For what value are customers willing to pay?
6. **Key Resources** - What key resources do our value propositions require?
7. **Key Activities** - What key activities do our value propositions require?
8. **Key Partnerships** - Who are our key partners and suppliers?
9. **Cost Structure** - What are the most important costs in our business model?

### 1.3 When to Apply in Life OS

**Primary Integration Points**:

- **Step 1 (Collect Ideas)**: Use BMC when the idea involves creating a business, service, or monetizable project
- **Step 4 (Consilium)**: Apply when experts need a structured canvas to evaluate business viability
- **Step 5 (Scoring)**: Score based on BMC completeness and strength of each building block
- **Step 8 (Deep Plan)**: Build comprehensive BMC as strategic foundation for execution

**Trigger Keywords/Patterns**:
- "Start a business", "launch a product", "monetize", "business model"
- "Revenue streams", "customers", "value proposition"
- Project type: entrepreneurial, commercial, startup-related

### 1.4 Life OS Integration Strategy

**Step 1 Integration** (Collect Ideas):
```yaml

  - If idea mentions: business/startup/revenue/customers
  - Suggest: "Consider using BMC to structure this business idea"
  - Quick BMC: Fill 2-3 key blocks (Value Prop, Customer Segments, Revenue)
```

**Step 5 Integration** (Scoring):

## 2. OKR (Objectives and Key Results)

### 2.1 Core Essence

**Origins**: Developed by Andy Grove at Intel, popularized by John Doerr (who introduced it to Google in 1999)
**Publication**: "Measure What Matters" by John Doerr (2018)

OKRs are a goal-setting framework that helps teams connect strategy to measurable outcomes by setting ambitious goals (Objectives) and tracking specific metrics (Key Results) to prove they're achieving them.

**Core Philosophy**: Focus on outcomes, not outputs. Set ambitious goals that stretch the team while remaining measurable and time-bound.

### 2.2 Structure of OKRs

**Objective**: Qualitative, inspirational goal answering "What do we want to achieve?"
- Should be ambitious, memorable, and motivating
- Example: "Become the go-to platform for remote team collaboration"

**Key Results**: 3-5 quantitative metrics answering "How will we know we're making progress?"
- Must be specific, time-bound, and measurable
- Example KRs for above Objective:
  - Increase active users from 10K to 50K by Q4
  - Achieve 4.5+ star rating on app stores (from 3.8)
  - Reduce customer churn from 12% to 5%

### 2.3 When to Apply in Life OS

**Primary Integration Points**:

- **Step 5 (Scoring)**: Translate project goals into OKRs for clarity and measurability
- **Step 6 (Integration)**: Align project OKRs with existing life goals and priorities
- **Step 8 (Deep Plan)**: Define quarterly OKRs as the execution roadmap
- **Evaluate Steps (Update Project, Rescoring)**: Track OKR progress and adjust

**Trigger Keywords/Patterns**:
- "Goal", "achieve", "improve", "increase", "outcome"
- Projects requiring measurable progress tracking
- Long-term projects (3+ months) needing milestones

### 2.4 Life OS Integration Strategy

**Step 5 Integration** (Scoring):
```yaml
okr_definition:
  - For high-scoring ideas (>3.5/5), require OKR formulation
  - Objective: Translate project vision into inspiring goal
  - Key Results: Define 3-5 measurable success metrics
  - Scoring boost: +0.5 if OKRs are well-defined
```

**Step 6 Integration** (Calendar Sync & Prioritization):
```yaml
alignment_check:
  - Map project OKRs to existing annual/quarterly OKRs
  - Conflict detection: Flag if project KRs contradict existing commitments
  - Resource allocation: Estimate time needed per KR
  - Prioritization: Higher-aligned OKRs get priority slots
```

**Step 8 Integration** (Deep Plan):
```yaml
okr_roadmap:
  - Break Objective into quarterly milestones
  - Assign Key Results to specific months/weeks
  - Weekly check-ins: Track KR progress (0-100%)
  - Monthly reviews: Adjust KRs if needed (within 20% variance)
  - End-of-quarter: Score OKR (0.0-1.0, where 0.7 = success)
```

## 3. SWOT Analysis (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats)

### 3.1 Core Essence

**Origins**: Developed in the 1960s-70s at Stanford Research Institute, attributed to Albert Humphrey
**Purpose**: Strategic planning tool for identifying internal and external factors affecting a project, business, or decision

SWOT helps you assess a situation from different perspectives and make better decisions about what to do next. It transforms observations into a clear plan for action and execution.

**Core Philosophy**: Holistic situation analysis combining internal capabilities (Strengths, Weaknesses) with external forces (Opportunities, Threats).

### 3.2 The Four Quadrants

**Internal Factors (within your control)**:
- **Strengths**: Internal attributes and resources that support success
- **Weaknesses**: Internal limitations or areas needing improvement

**External Factors (outside your control)**:
- **Opportunities**: External factors you can capitalize on or use to your advantage
- **Threats**: External challenges or risks that could cause problems

### 3.3 When to Apply in Life OS

**Primary Integration Points**:

- **Step 1 (Collect Ideas)**: Quick SWOT to validate idea feasibility
- **Step 2 (Roles Discovery)**: Identify which specialists can address weaknesses/threats
- **Step 4 (Consilium)**: Experts contribute to SWOT from different perspectives
- **Step 5 (Scoring)**: Use SWOT to inform scoring criteria (strong S+O = higher score)
- **Step 8 (Deep Plan)**: Comprehensive SWOT to inform strategy and risk mitigation

**Trigger Keywords/Patterns**:
- "Feasibility", "risks", "challenges", "advantages", "competition"
- Decision-making scenarios (choose between options)
- Projects with uncertainty or competitive landscape
- Career decisions (job change, skill development)

### 3.4 Life OS Integration Strategy

**Step 1 Integration** (Collect Ideas):
```yaml
quick_swot:
  purpose: "Rapid feasibility check for new ideas"
  time: 5-10 minutes
  depth: 2-3 items per quadrant
  decision:
    - If Threats > Opportunities AND Weaknesses > Strengths → Flag as "High Risk"
    - If Strengths + Opportunities dominate → Fast-track to Consilium
```

**Step 4 Integration** (Consilium):
```yaml
multi_expert_swot:
  process:
    - Each specialist contributes SWOT from their domain
    - Finance expert: Financial strengths/weaknesses
    - Marketing expert: Market opportunities/threats
    - Technical expert: Technical capabilities/limitations
  synthesis:
    - Merge all SWOTs into master SWOT
    - Identify patterns (what appears in multiple expert SWOTs?)
    - Prioritize top 3-5 items per quadrant
```

**Step 5 Integration** (Scoring):
```yaml
swot_scoring:
  formula:
    - Strength Score: (# of strengths × strength impact) / 10
    - Opportunity Score: (# of opportunities × likelihood) / 10
    - Weakness Penalty: -1 × (# of critical weaknesses × 0.5)
    - Threat Penalty: -1 × (# of severe threats × threat probability)

  final_swot_score: (Strength + Opportunity + Weakness_Penalty + Threat_Penalty) / 2
  weight: 0.20  # SWOT contributes 20% to overall project score
```

**Step 8 Integration** (Deep Plan):
```yaml
swot_strategy_mapping:
  SO_Strategies: "Use Strengths to capitalize on Opportunities"
  WO_Strategies: "Address Weaknesses to capture Opportunities"
  ST_Strategies: "Use Strengths to mitigate Threats"
  WT_Strategies: "Defensive plan to minimize Weaknesses and avoid Threats"

  risk_mitigation: "For each Threat, define prevention or contingency plan"
  capability_building: "For each Weakness, define improvement action"
```

## 4. Porter's Five Forces

### 4.1 Core Essence

**Creator**: Michael Porter (Harvard Business School)
**Publication**: "Competitive Strategy" (1979) - later refined in "The Five Forces" (2008)

Porter's Five Forces is a framework for analyzing the competitive structure of an industry and its profitability. It helps understand the factors that affect competition and strategic positioning within a market.

**Core Philosophy**: Industry structure determines competitive behavior, which in turn determines profitability. Understanding these forces helps businesses position themselves advantageously.

### 4.2 The Five Forces

**Three sources of Horizontal Competition**:
1. **Threat of New Entrants** - How easy is it for new competitors to enter?
2. **Threat of Substitute Products/Services** - Can customers switch to alternatives?
3. **Rivalry Among Existing Competitors** - How intense is current competition?

**Two sources of Vertical Competition**:
4. **Bargaining Power of Suppliers** - How much leverage do suppliers have?
5. **Bargaining Power of Buyers** - How much leverage do customers have?

### 4.3 When to Apply in Life OS

**Primary Integration Points**:

- **Step 1 (Collect Ideas)**: Apply when idea involves entering a market or industry
- **Step 4 (Consilium)**: Business/market experts analyze competitive forces
- **Step 5 (Scoring)**: Score based on industry attractiveness (weak forces = higher score)
- **Step 8 (Deep Plan)**: Comprehensive Five Forces analysis to inform competitive strategy

```yaml
five_forces_scoring:
  formula:
    - Industry_Attractiveness = (5 - avg_force_strength) / 5
    - Where force strength: 1=Weak (favorable), 5=Strong (unfavorable)

  scoring:
    - Rate each force: 1-5 (1=low threat/power, 5=high threat/power)
    - Average all 5 forces
    - Inverse score (lower average = more attractive industry)

  weight: 0.15  # Five Forces contributes 15% to overall project score

  interpretation:
    - Avg 1.0-2.0: Highly attractive industry (weak competitive forces)
    - Avg 2.1-3.0: Moderately attractive (balanced forces)
    - Avg 3.1-4.0: Challenging industry (strong forces)
    - Avg 4.1-5.0: Unattractive industry (intense competition, avoid)
```

```yaml
    - Focus: Niche market to avoid rivalry and new entrants

    - Watch for shifts (e.g., new technology lowers entry barriers)
```

### 4.6 Synergies with Creative Frameworks

**With TRIZ**:
- Use TRIZ to **reduce force strength** (e.g., apply "Segmentation" to create niche and avoid rivalry)
- Apply Inventive Principles to **build barriers to entry** (Principle 10: Prior Action)
- Use Trends of Evolution to **predict industry shifts** (how will forces evolve?)

**With Six Thinking Hats**:
- White Hat: Gather data on each force (market research, competitor analysis)
- Red Hat: Gut feelings about industry attractiveness (emotional response to competition)
- Black Hat: Critical assessment of threats (focus on strong forces)
- Yellow Hat: Optimistic view of opportunities (focus on weak forces)
- Green Hat: Creative strategies to mitigate strong forces or exploit weak ones
- Blue Hat: Process control (ensure thorough analysis of all 5 forces)

**With SCAMPER**:
- **Substitute**: Find substitute suppliers or distribution channels to reduce their power
- **Combine**: Merge with suppliers/buyers to reduce their power (vertical integration)
- **Adapt**: Adopt strategies from industries with similar force profiles
- **Modify**: Modify business model to shift force balance (e.g., marketplace model)
- **Put to other use**: Use force strength to your advantage (e.g., high rivalry = partnerships)
- **Eliminate**: Remove dependency on powerful suppliers or buyers
- **Reverse**: Turn force weakness into strength (e.g., educate fragmented buyers to increase their power, then serve them)

**With SWOT**:
- **SWOT ↔ Five Forces Mapping**:
  - Weak forces → Opportunities (attractive market)
  - Strong forces → Threats (difficult competitive environment)
  - Internal capabilities → Strengths to counter forces
  - Resource gaps → Weaknesses when facing strong forces

**With OKRs**:
- Define Objectives to address strongest forces
- Set Key Results to track force mitigation (e.g., "Reduce supplier concentration from 3 to 10 vendors")

## 5. Lean Canvas

### 5.1 Core Essence

**Creator**: Ash Maurya (adapted from Business Model Canvas)
**Publication**: "Running Lean" (2012)

Lean Canvas is a one-page business model framework optimized for startups and innovation projects. It's an adaptation of the Business Model Canvas that emphasizes problems, solutions, key metrics, and unfair advantage - critical elements for lean startup methodology.

**Core Philosophy**: Test before building. Create hypotheses, validate with customers quickly, pivot based on learning. Focus on problem-solution fit before product-market fit.

### 5.2 The 9 Lean Canvas Blocks

**Customer-focused (right side)**:
1. **Customer Segments** - Who are your early adopters?
2. **Problem** - Top 3 problems worth solving
3. **Existing Alternatives** - How customers solve these problems today

**Solution-focused (left side)**:
4. **Solution** - Top 3 features addressing the problems
5. **Unique Value Proposition** - Single, clear, compelling message
6. **Channels** - Path to customers

**Business model (bottom)**:
7. **Revenue Streams** - How you'll make money
8. **Cost Structure** - What it costs to operate

**Competitive advantage (top)**:
9. **Key Metrics** - Numbers that matter most
10. **Unfair Advantage** - Something that can't be easily copied

### 5.3 When to Apply in Life OS

**Primary Integration Points**:

- **Step 1 (Collect Ideas)**: Use Lean Canvas for startup/business ideas requiring validation
- **Step 3 (Specialist Match)**: Match to customer development and lean startup experts
- **Step 5 (Scoring)**: Score based on problem intensity, solution viability, and unfair advantage
- **Step 8 (Deep Plan)**: Full Lean Canvas becomes the execution blueprint

**Trigger Keywords/Patterns**:
- "Startup", "new product", "innovation", "MVP", "validate"
- "Customer problem", "product-market fit", "early adopters"
- Projects requiring iterative testing and pivoting
- Side hustles and entrepreneurial ventures

### 5.4 Life OS Integration Strategy

**Step 1 Integration** (Collect Ideas):
```yaml
lean_canvas_quick:
  time: 10-15 minutes
  focus:
    - Problem: What 3 problems are you solving?
    - Customer Segments: Who has these problems badly?
    - Unique Value Proposition: Why you and why now?

  decision:
    - If problem is "nice to have" → Deprioritize
    - If problem is "urgent and important" → Fast-track to validation
    - If target customers are unknown → Require customer discovery first
```

**Step 5 Integration** (Scoring):
```yaml
lean_canvas_scoring:
  problem_intensity: # How badly do customers feel this problem?
    - Score 1-5: (1=nice to have, 5=urgent/expensive problem)
    - Weight: 30%

  solution_fit: # How well does solution address problem?
    - Score 1-5: (1=tangential, 5=perfect fit)
    - Weight: 20%

  unfair_advantage: # How defensible is this?
    - Score 1-5: (1=none, 5=can't be copied)
    - Weight: 20%

  customer_access: # Can you reach early adopters?
    - Score 1-5: (1=no access, 5=direct access)
    - Weight: 15%

  revenue_model: # Is monetization clear?
    - Score 1-5: (1=unclear, 5=validated willingness to pay)
    - Weight: 15%

  total_score: (problem_intensity × 0.3) + (solution_fit × 0.2) +
               (unfair_advantage × 0.2) + (customer_access × 0.15) +
               (revenue_model × 0.15)
```

**Step 8 Integration** (Deep Plan):
```yaml
lean_canvas_execution:
  phase_1_problem_validation: # Weeks 1-2
    - Interview 10-20 potential customers
    - Validate top 3 problems exist and are urgent
    - Identify early adopter segment
    - Update canvas based on learnings

  phase_2_solution_validation: # Weeks 3-4
    - Build MVP (minimum viable product)
    - Test with 5-10 early adopters
    - Measure key metric (activation, retention, etc.)
    - Update solution and UVP based on feedback

  phase_3_business_model_validation: # Weeks 5-8
    - Test pricing and revenue model
    - Validate channels (can you acquire customers profitably?)
    - Measure unit economics (LTV > 3x CAC)
    - Pivot if needed based on metrics

  iteration_cycle: "Every 2 weeks, update canvas and decide: Persevere, Pivot, or Perish"
```

**With BMC**:

### 5.7 Time Estimates

**Important**: Don't spend too long on initial canvas! It's meant to be a living document that evolves weekly based on learning.

## 6. Value Proposition Canvas

### 6.1 Core Essence

**Creator**: Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur (companion tool to Business Model Canvas)
**Publication**: "Value Proposition Design" (2014)

The Value Proposition Canvas is a tool for achieving product-market fit by deeply understanding customer needs and designing solutions that precisely address them. It takes the "Value Proposition" and "Customer Segments" boxes from the Business Model Canvas and examines them in detail.

**Core Philosophy**: Before designing solutions, deeply understand the customer's world - their jobs, pains, and gains. Then design value propositions that relieve pains and create gains. Fit is achieved when customers care about the value you create.

### 6.2 The Two-Sided Canvas

**RIGHT SIDE: Customer Profile** (understand the customer)
1. **Customer Jobs** - Tasks customers are trying to get done (functional, social, emotional)
2. **Pains** - Negative experiences, emotions, risks, obstacles
3. **Gains** - Benefits customers expect, desire, or would be delighted by

**LEFT SIDE: Value Map** (design your offering)
4. **Products & Services** - What you offer
5. **Pain Relievers** - How your offering reduces or eliminates pains
6. **Gain Creators** - How your offering creates customer gains

**FIT = Pain Relievers address important Pains + Gain Creators deliver desired Gains**

### 6.3 When to Apply in Life OS

**Primary Integration Points**:

- **Step 1 (Collect Ideas)**: Use when idea involves serving specific customers/users
- **Step 2 (Roles Discovery)**: Identify need for customer research/user experience specialists
- **Step 4 (Consilium)**: Customer-facing experts contribute to Customer Profile
- **Step 5 (Scoring)**: Score based on strength of fit between Value Map and Customer Profile
- **Step 8 (Deep Plan)**: Comprehensive VPC ensures product-market fit before building

**Trigger Keywords/Patterns**:
- "Customer needs", "user pain points", "value proposition", "product-market fit"
- Projects involving product/service design
- Career transitions (you are the "product" being positioned)
- Content creation (audience = customers)

### 6.4 Life OS Integration Strategy

**Step 1 Integration** (Collect Ideas):
```yaml
quick_vpc_check:
  questions:
    - "Who is this for?" (Customer Segment)
    - "What job are they trying to do?" (Customer Jobs)
    - "What makes that job painful right now?" (Pains)
    - "What would make it way better?" (Gains)

  decision:
    - If customer jobs are unclear → Flag: "Needs customer discovery"
    - If pains are minor → Flag: "Low-value problem"
    - If gains are specific and urgent → Fast-track to validation
```

**Step 5 Integration** (Scoring):
```yaml
vpc_fit_scoring:
  pain_relief_score:
    - Count critical pains (ranked by customer)
    - Count pain relievers that address them
    - Score: (pain_relievers_matched / critical_pains) × 5
    - Weight: 40%

  gain_creation_score:
    - Count essential/expected gains (ranked by customer)
    - Count gain creators that deliver them
    - Score: (gain_creators_matched / essential_gains) × 5
    - Weight: 30%

  product_service_alignment:
    - Score 1-5: How well do your products/services map to customer jobs?
    - Weight: 30%

  total_vpc_score: (pain_relief × 0.4) + (gain_creation × 0.3) + (alignment × 0.3)

  fit_interpretation:
    - 4.0-5.0: Strong fit (product-market fit likely)
    - 3.0-3.9: Moderate fit (needs iteration)
    - 2.0-2.9: Weak fit (significant redesign needed)
    - <2.0: No fit (wrong problem or wrong solution)
```

**Step 8 Integration** (Deep Plan):
```yaml
vpc_validation_roadmap:
  phase_1_customer_profile: # Weeks 1-2
    - Interview 10-20 customers to map jobs, pains, gains
    - Rank pains (extreme, severe, moderate) and gains (essential, expected, desired)
    - Identify most underserved customer jobs

  phase_2_value_map_design: # Weeks 3-4
    - Design products/services addressing top customer jobs
    - Create pain relievers for top 3 pains
    - Create gain creators for top 3 gains
    - Prioritize features by pain/gain intensity

  phase_3_fit_testing: # Weeks 5-8
    - Show value map to customers, measure excitement
    - Test messaging: Do customers understand the value?
    - MVP testing: Does the product actually relieve pains/create gains?
    - Iterate based on feedback

  success_metric: "80% of interviewed customers say 'I would definitely use/buy this'"
```

### 6.6 Synergies with Creative Frameworks

**With TRIZ**:
- Use TRIZ **Ideality** to strengthen Value Proposition (increase gains, reduce pains, minimize costs)
- Apply Inventive Principles to **create unexpected gains** (delight customers)
- Use Contradiction Matrix: "How to maximize Gain X while minimizing Pain Y?"

**With Six Thinking Hats**:
- White Hat: Gather facts from customer interviews (what do they actually say?)
- Red Hat: Emotional response to pains and gains (what frustrates them most?)
- Black Hat: Critical evaluation (are we addressing the RIGHT pains? Real gains?)
- Yellow Hat: Optimistic view of opportunity (how big is this market if we nail the fit?)
- Green Hat: Creative alternatives for pain relievers and gain creators
- Blue Hat: Process control (ensure Customer Profile comes from real customers, not assumptions)

**With SCAMPER**:
- **Substitute**: Substitute existing pain relievers with better ones
- **Combine**: Combine multiple gain creators into one powerful offering
- **Adapt**: Adapt solutions from other industries to create unexpected gains
- **Modify**: Modify features to better address pains
- **Put to other use**: Repurpose existing capabilities to create new gains
- **Eliminate**: Remove features that don't relieve pains or create gains (focus!)
- **Reverse**: Flip a pain into a gain (e.g., "takes time" → "therapeutic ritual")

**With Lean Canvas**:
- Customer Profile → directly informs **Problem** and **Customer Segments**
- Value Map → directly informs **Solution** and **Unique Value Proposition**
- Use VPC to validate Lean Canvas assumptions

**With BMC**:
- Customer Profile → informs **Customer Segments** and **Customer Relationships**
- Value Map → informs **Value Propositions**
- Pain Relievers/Gain Creators → shape **Key Activities** and **Key Resources**

| **Step 5: Scoring** | OKRs, SWOT, Lean Canvas | All frameworks contribute to scoring | Quantify project viability and potential |
| **Step 6: Integration** | OKRs | SWOT | Align with existing goals and priorities |
| **Step 8: Deep Plan** | OKRs (roadmap), Lean Canvas OR BMC | VPC, Porter's Five Forces | Comprehensive execution blueprint |
| **Evaluate: Update Project** | OKRs (track KRs) | Lean Canvas (iterate) | Measure progress and adapt |
| **Evaluate: Rescoring** | SWOT (updated), OKRs (progress) | Porter's Five Forces (market shifts) | Re-evaluate based on learnings |

### 1. Progressive Disclosure

Don't overwhelm users with all 6 frameworks upfront. Recommend based on:
- Project type detected (keywords, category)
- Step in workflow (Step 1 vs. Step 8)
- User experience level (beginner vs. advanced)

### 2. Template Library

Provide pre-filled examples for each framework across common project types:
- Startup launch
- Career transition
- Side project
- Content creation
- Skill development
- Business expansion

### 3. Framework Combinations

Pre-configure framework "stacks" for common scenarios:
- **Lean Startup Stack**: Lean Canvas + VPC + OKRs
- **Career Strategy Stack**: SWOT + VPC + OKRs
- **Business Analysis Stack**: Porter's Five Forces + SWOT + BMC
- **Product Design Stack**: VPC + Lean Canvas + OKRs

### 4. Auto-Scoring Integration

Build scoring algorithms that:
- Extract data from frameworks automatically
- Weight based on project type and stage
- Flag critical gaps (e.g., "No pain relievers for top customer pain")
- Suggest next actions (e.g., "Run customer interviews to validate Problem")

### 5. Cross-Framework Validation

Ensure consistency across frameworks:
- VPC "Customer Jobs" should align with Lean Canvas "Problem"
- BMC "Value Proposition" should match VPC "Pain Relievers + Gain Creators"
- OKR Key Results should measure progress on framework hypotheses

## Conclusion

These 6 business and career frameworks provide comprehensive coverage of strategic thinking for Life OS:

1. **Business Model Canvas** - Overall business model design
2. **OKRs** - Goal-setting and execution tracking
3. **SWOT** - Situation analysis and strategic positioning
4. **Porter's Five Forces** - Industry and competitive analysis
5. **Lean Canvas** - Lean startup methodology and hypothesis testing
6. **Value Proposition Canvas** - Customer-centric value design

**Key Insights**:
- All frameworks are highly synergistic and complement each other
- OKRs and Lean Canvas are most universally applicable
- VPC provides deepest customer understanding
- Porter's Five Forces is essential for market entry decisions
- SWOT is quickest for high-level assessment
- BMC is best for established businesses needing clarity

**Recommended Integration**:
- Use **2-3 frameworks minimum** per project for comprehensive analysis
- Start with **quick versions** in Step 1-5, go deep in Step 8
- Combine with **TRIZ, Six Hats, and SCAMPER** for creative problem-solving
- **Iterate frameworks** based on learnings (living documents)
