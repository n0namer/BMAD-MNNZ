## WORD-LEVEL ANTI-PATTERNS

### Overused Formal Words (Latinate Bias)

**AI Preference (Professional Register):**
- utilize (use)
- facilitate (help)
- demonstrate (show)
- serves as (is)
- features/offers (has)
- embark on (start)
- navigate (deal with)

**Why this happens:**
- RLHF training teaches models to sound "authoritative"
- English associates authority with Latinate words
- Creates permanent "business casual" register
- Never varies between registers like humans do

**Human Alternative:**
- Mix fancy words WITH simple ones
- Use monosyllabic Germanic words: show, use, help, make, take, give, have
- Vary register: casual + formal + technical as needed
- Short punchy sentences next to complex ones

### Overused Abstract Words (Disembodied Vocabulary)

**AI Characteristics:**
- Abstract conceptual words vs concrete tangible ones
- Words that can't be visualized
- No mental image possible from reading it

**Common AI Words:**
- comprehensive, foundational, nuanced, landscape
- realm, tapestry, beacon, cacophony
- paradigm, dynamic, vital, crucial

**Why this happens:**
- AI has read everything but experienced nothing
- Drawing from statistical average of language
- Easier to write abstracts about big topics than specifics about small topics

**The Fix:**
- Use concrete, visualizable language
- "3 hours saved per week" vs "time optimization benefits"
- "Customer felt relieved" vs "experienced enhanced satisfaction"
- Paint pictures, don't make generalizations

### Hedging Language (Uncertainty Signals)

**AI's Safety Protocols Create Cautious Tone:**

| Phrase | Meaning | Better Alternative |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| it could be argued | vague possibility | Direct statement with evidence |
| tends to | recurring pattern | "Usually..." or direct claim |
| typically | general pattern | Specific examples |
| generally speaking | vague generalization | Make a specific claim |
| to some extent | hedging | Full commitment or disclaimer |
| from a broader perspective | perspective hedging | Show your actual perspective |
| it's important to note | emphasis hedge | Just state it directly |

**Why AI does this:**
- Trained to be "helpful and harmless"
- Avoids making statements that might be wrong
- Creates uniform caution that becomes predictable
- Reader can sense the hesitation

**Impact:**
- Makes writing feel perpetually wishy-washy
- Strips writing of conviction and authority
- Makes prose less compelling and persuasive
- Signals that writer doesn't believe what they're saying

**Human approach:**
- Vary certainty based on evidence
- Strong claims where supported
- Honest disclaimers where appropriate
- Never uniformly cautious

## SENTENCE-LEVEL ANTI-PATTERNS

### The Em-Dash Problem (ChatGPT Dash)

**How it appears:**
- Over 2-5x more common than in human text
- Used as universal punctuation solution
- Connects ideas, adds emphasis, introduces explanations

**Example (AI):**
"The system analyzes data—revealing key insights—and provides recommendations—enabling better decisions."

**Example (Human):**
"The system analyzes data, revealing key insights. This enables better decisions."

**Why AI does this:**
- em-dash feels like "professional" punctuation
- Mimics "punched up" sales-like writing style
- Mode collapse: overuse of high-probability pattern

### Template Sentence Structures

**"From X to Y" Construction (Repeated):**
- "From beginners to experts"
- "From small startups to Fortune 500 companies"
- "From concept to reality"

**Why AI overuses:**
- High probability pattern in training data
- Feels like comprehensive coverage
- Easy to generate variations

**Human approach:**
- Use sparingly, not as go-to structure
- Vary sentence construction patterns
- Let natural variation happen

### Present Participial Phrases (2-5x more common)

**Pattern:** Main clause + comma + -ing verb phrase

**Example (AI):**
"The company provides solutions, enabling clients to streamline workflows while reducing costs."

**Why AI overuses:**
- Feels sophisticated and clear
- Instruction-tuned models trained on this pattern
- Mimics professional writing style

**Better approach (Human):**
"The company provides solutions. Clients streamline workflows and reduce costs."

### Uniform Sentence Length (The Metronome Effect)

**AI Characteristic:**
- Consistently 12-18 words per sentence
- Predictable rhythm throughout
- No variation to create emphasis

**Measure in your copy:**
- Count words in each sentence
- If all within same 5-word range → Sounds AI
- If mixture of 5 to 30+ words → Sounds human

**Fix:**
- Mix super short with super long
- "It works." vs "The comprehensive nature of the implementation allows for seamless integration across multiple platforms."
- Create rhythm through variation

### Repetitive Transition Phrases

**AI Behavior:**
- Opens consecutive paragraphs with same connector
- "Furthermore" used 15x more than human writing
- Overdependence on: moreover, additionally, consequently

**Why it's a problem:**
- Reads like debate team outline
- Signals robotic structure
- Removes natural narrative flow

**Human approach:**
- Delete 50% of transitions entirely
- Let ideas flow by proximity
- Use casual pivots: "Still," "On top of that," "Even so"
- Vary connectors across paragraphs

### "Rule of Three" Overuse

**Pattern:** Three of the same structure repeated

**Example (AI):**
"The platform is secure, scalable, and sustainable."
"From bustling cities to serene landscapes to remote villages."
"We deliver on time, within budget, and with quality."

**Why AI overuses:**
- Feels comprehensive and balanced
- Makes shallow analysis seem thorough
- High-probability pattern in training data

**Better approach:**
- Use three items when genuinely necessary
- Don't force artificial balance
- Vary structure: sometimes 2, sometimes 4, sometimes narrative

## PARAGRAPH-LEVEL ANTI-PATTERNS

### Perfect Structural Symmetry

**AI Characteristic:**
- Each paragraph roughly same length
- Each ends with neat conclusion
- Predictable, box-like structure

**Example (AI):**
- Paragraph 1: 4 sentences, conclusion
- Paragraph 2: 4 sentences, conclusion
- Paragraph 3: 4 sentences, conclusion

**Human reality:**
- Some 1-sentence paragraphs for emphasis
- Some 8+ sentence paragraphs for complex ideas
- Organic structure, not symmetric

**Fix:**
- Deliberately vary paragraph length
- Use 1-sentence paragraphs for punch
- Use multi-sentence for complexity
- Visual variety on page

### Mechanical Formatting

**AI Overuse:**
- Excessive boldface (every key term bolded)
- Bullet points on every list
- Unnecessary tables that should be prose
- Identical heading hierarchy

**Why AI does this:**
- Trained on READMEs, wikis, sales pages (heavy bolding)
- Inherited from instructional text training data
- Feels "organized" and "professional"

**Human approach:**
- Bold sparingly for emphasis
- Use punctuation and line breaks for rhythm
- Prose when it makes sense
- Tables only when truly helpful

## TEXT-LEVEL ANTI-PATTERNS

### Statistical Regression to the Mean

**AI Behavior:**
- Generic statements that apply to many topics
- Smoothing specifics into generalizations
- Loss of concrete details into abstraction

**Example (AI):**
"Technology has transformed modern business, creating opportunities while presenting challenges that require strategic approaches."

**Translation:** This could apply to literally anything.

**Example (Human):**
"Customer service teams now use AI to respond to 70% of tickets, freeing humans to handle 30% of complex issues. But we've also seen response times drop by 15% because the AI sometimes misunderstands context."

**Why this happens:**
- AI draws from statistical average of training data
- Specific facts get averaged into vagueness
- Easier to generate generic than specific

### Hallucinated Facts and Fabricated Citations

**AI Signature:**
- Oddly specific data points with no source
- Precise statistics that don't exist
- Citations to studies that can't be verified
- Broken URLs in "sources"

**Examples:**
- "A 2023 Pew survey found 73.4% of Gen Z prefer analog watches"
- "According to Harvard Business Review research from March 2024..."
- Links that 404 when clicked

**Why it happens:**
- Predictive model creates plausible-sounding facts
- No ground truth verification
- Rewarded by RLHF for being specific

**The fix:**
- Verify EVERY claim
- If not found in 2 minutes, remove or rewrite
- Credit sources you actually read
- Lose word count rather than credibility

### Absence of Lived Experience

**AI Characteristic:**
- Text is too abstract to visualize
- No personal perspective or insight
- Generalized statements about universal experience
- Can't imagine actual use case

**Example (AI):**
"The application of advanced methodologies facilitates enhanced operational efficiency through systematic implementation of best practices."

**Translation:** I have no idea what this means concretely.

**Example (Human):**
"I spent 3 hours scheduling my team's calendar each week. Then I switched systems and that dropped to 15 minutes. Here's why: the new tool learns your patterns..."

**Why AI does this:**
- No personal experience or sensory memory
- Draws from patterns, not actual usage
- Can't access embodied knowledge

## DETECTION CHECKLIST

### Quick Scan (60 seconds)

**Read aloud and check:**
- [ ] Tongue trips over phrases → AI likely
- [ ] Every paragraph roughly same length → AI likely
- [ ] "Furthermore," "moreover," "additionally" repeated → AI likely
- [ ] No specific examples anywhere → AI likely
- [ ] Feels emotionally flat → AI likely
- [ ] Can't visualize what it's describing → AI likely
- [ ] Could be about anyone → AI likely
- [ ] Contains "delve," "testament," "realm," "serves as" → AI likely
- [ ] Every sentence 12-18 words → AI likely
- [ ] Perfect structure, no messiness → AI likely

### Deeper Analysis (5 minutes)

**Count and measure:**
- Transition words per paragraph (AI overuses)
- Sentence length variation (AI uniform)
- Hedging words: "tends to," "typically," "could" (AI overuses)
- Personal pronouns: I, you, we (AI underuses)
- Specific details vs generalizations
- Citations verified vs claimed
- Contractions used (AI underuses)

### Level Assessment

**Definitely human:**
- Personal anecdote with specific details
- Strong opinions stated directly
- Varied sentence length and rhythm
- Personality evident throughout
- Specific examples from own experience
- Some messiness and tangents
- Emotional authenticity

**Likely human:**
- Most characteristics above
- Some hedging (but not excessive)
- Generally conversational
- Shows perspective
- Verifiable claims

**Likely AI:**
- Multiple anti-patterns present
- Formal register throughout
- Generic statements
- Hedging language common
- No personality evident
- Perfect structure
- Technical perfection

**Definitely AI:**
- Multiple anti-patterns stacked
- Phrases from overused list (3+ phrases)
- Hallucinated facts
- Self-referential ("As an AI...")
- Perfect uniformity
- Emotionally flat throughout
- Every characteristic above

## DIFFERENT MODEL "AIDIOLECTS" (Writing Fingerprints)

Research shows different AI models have distinct vocabulary preferences:

**ChatGPT Tendencies:**
- Overuses: "intricate," "underscore," "in the ever-evolving"
- Em-dash frequency: Highest
- Hedge words: Moderate

**Gemini Tendencies:**
- Overuses: "palpable," "continuation"
- Structural predictability: Medium
- Academic formality: High

**Claude Tendencies:**
- Longer, more varied sentences
- Fewer obvious anti-patterns
- More conversational than academic
- Still mode collapses on certain phrases

**Key insight:** Different models create different "fingerprints" in writing patterns.

## PROBABILITY SCORING (Not Perfect, But Useful)

**If checking with AI detection tools, interpret results:**

**0-20% AI likelihood:**
- Probably genuinely human
- May have one or two AI-like phrases
- Overall authentic

**20-50% AI likelihood:**
- Could be human, could be hybrid
- May have been AI-assisted heavily
- Needs closer reading

**50-80% AI likelihood:**
- Probably AI-generated
- May be heavily edited AI output
- Shows multiple anti-patterns

**80-100% AI likelihood:**
- Likely AI-generated
- Shows most anti-patterns
- Verify with manual reading

**CAVEAT:** These tools falsely flag 20-30% of human writing as AI. Don't rely on tools alone. Manual reading catches what tools miss.

## FALSE POSITIVE RISKS

### Who Gets Flagged as AI (But Isn't)

- **Non-native English speakers** (can sound formulaic)
- **Academic writing** (formal, hedged, structured)
- **Technical documentation** (repetitive, formal)
- **Corporate writing** (uniform register)
- **Students trained to write formally** (often penalized)
- **ESL (English as Second Language) writers** (up to 70% false positive rate)

### Why This Matters

- Over-reliance on AI detection tools is discriminatory
- Manual human reading catches nuance
- Context matters more than patterns
- One anti-pattern isn't proof of AI

## HOW HUMANS GET IT RIGHT

**What distinguishes genuinely human writing:**

1. **Specific, verifiable details** (not generic)
2. **Personal perspective** evident (not neutral)
3. **Varied sentence structure** (not metronome)
4. **Emotional authenticity** (not forced)
5. **Admitted limitations** (not claimed perfection)
6. **Concrete examples** (not abstractions)
7. **Messiness** (strategic imperfection)
8. **Conversational flow** (reads like speech)
9. **Personality markers** (unique voice)
10. **Connection to lived experience** (grounded, not theoretical)
